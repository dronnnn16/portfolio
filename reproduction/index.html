Why do organisms reproduce?

Reproduction (जनन) is not needed for an individual to stay alive. It is needed for the species to continue.

DNA copying डीएनए प्रतिकृति

Exam point. The basic event in reproduction is making a copy of DNA.

DNA in the chromosomes of the nucleus carries the information for making proteins, and proteins decide the body design. A cell copies its DNA with chemical reactions, makes extra cell apparatus, and then divides into two cells.

Variation विभिन्नता

Exam point. DNA copying is never 100% accurate, so small variations appear. Variation helps a species survive when the environment changes.

If water temperature rises, most bacteria in a population may die, but a few variants that resist heat survive and grow. Variation is useful for the species, not always for the individual. Too much change in a DNA copy can stop the new cell from working.

Population and niche

Exam point. Consistent DNA copying keeps the body design suited to the organism's niche (habitat).

Reproduction keeps populations stable. Changes in the niche are outside the organism's control, which is why some variation is needed.

Modes of asexual reproduction

A single parent produces offspring. No gametes are formed, and the young are near copies of the parent. Press play to watch each mode happen.

    Fission विखंडन

    Binary fission द्विखंडन: one cell splits into two equal halves. Amoeba (any plane), Leishmania (a definite plane, with respect to its whip-like flagellum).

    Multiple fission बहुखंडन: one cell divides into many daughter cells at the same time. Plasmodium, the malaria parasite.

    Leishmania causes kala-azar. In multiple fission the cell first forms a protective cyst.

    Fragmentation खंडन

    Exam point. A mature body breaks into pieces and each piece grows into a new individual. Example: Spirogyra.

    This works in simple multicellular organisms whose cells are not very specialised.

    Regeneration पुनर्जनन

    Exam point. A fully differentiated organism can grow a whole new body from a cut piece. Examples: Planaria, Hydra.

    Specialised cells divide and change into the different cell types and tissues. Regeneration is not the same as reproduction, because most organisms do not normally depend on being cut up. Complex organisms cannot regenerate because their cells are highly specialised and organised into organs.

    Budding मुकुलन

    Exam point. A bud grows out at one spot due to repeated cell division, becomes a small individual, and detaches when mature. Examples: Hydra, yeast.

    Spore formation बीजाणु समासंघ

    Exam point. Rhizopus (bread mould) forms blob-like sporangia on hyphae. Each sporangium holds spores that grow into new Rhizopus.

    Spores have thick walls that protect them until they reach a moist surface where they can grow.

    Vegetative propagation कायिक प्रवर्धन

    Exam point. New plants grow from roots, stems or leaves. Examples: Bryophyllum (buds in leaf notches), potato (stem tuber), sugarcane, rose, grapes, banana.

    • Artificial methods: layering, grafting, cutting.
    • Helps plants that have lost the capacity to produce seeds: banana, orange, rose, jasmine.
    • All new plants are genetically similar to the parent, so good traits are kept.
    • Plants bear flowers and fruits earlier than plants grown from seed.

    Tissue culture ऊतक संवर्धन

    Exam point. Cells from the growing tip of a plant are grown in an artificial nutrient medium to form a callus. Hormones then make the callus grow into many plantlets.

    Used to grow ornamental plants on a large scale. The plantlets are moved to pots or soil once they are strong enough.

    Sexual reproduction in flowering plants

    Sexual reproduction mixes DNA from two parents, so it creates more variation. Germ cells carry half the chromosomes, so the number stays constant over generations. Tap any part of the flower.

    Unisexual and bisexual flowers

    Exam point. Unisexual flowers have either stamens or pistil: papaya, watermelon. Bisexual flowers have both: Hibiscus, mustard.

    Pollination परागण

    Exam point. Transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma.

    • Self-pollination: to the stigma of the same flower.
    • Cross-pollination: to the stigma of another flower, by wind, water or animals such as insects.

    Fertilisation निषेचन

    Exam point. The male germ cell fuses with the female germ cell (egg) in the ovule to form a zygote (युग्मनज).

    A pollen tube grows out of the pollen grain, travels through the style and reaches the ovule in the ovary.

    After fertilisation

    Zygote divides to form the embryo.

    Ovule develops a tough coat and becomes the seed.

    Ovary grows and ripens into the fruit.

    Petals, sepals, stamens, style and stigma shrivel and fall off.

    Germination अंकुरण

    Exam point. The seed has the future plant (embryo), which grows into a seedling in suitable conditions.

    • Plumule: future shoot.
    • Radicle: future root.
    • Cotyledon: stores food for the embryo.

    Reproduction in human beings

    Humans reproduce sexually. The reproductive organs mature at puberty, when the body becomes able to make germ cells.

    Puberty किशोरावस्था / यौवनारंभ

    Exam point. In both: hair in armpits and genital area, thinner hair on legs, arms and face, oily skin and pimples.

    • Girls: breasts develop, skin of the nipple darkens, menstruation begins.
    • Boys: facial hair (beard and moustache), voice begins to crack.

    Male reproductive system

    Exam point. Testes make sperms and testosterone. They sit in the scrotum because sperm formation needs a lower temperature than the body.

    1. Testes (वृषण): sperms are formed.
    2. Vas deferens (शुक्रवाहिका): carries sperms; it loops over the bladder.
    3. Urethra (मूत्रमार्ग): common passage for sperms and urine.
    4. Penis (शिश्न): passes semen out.

    Seminal vesicles and the prostate gland add secretions that make transport easier and nourish the sperms. Sperms are tiny, carry genetic material, and have a long tail to swim towards the egg.

    Female reproductive system

    Exam point. Ovaries make eggs and hormones. One egg is released each month by one of the two ovaries.

    1. Ovaries (अंडाशय): a girl is born with thousands of immature eggs; they start maturing at puberty.
    2. Oviduct or fallopian tube (अंडवाहिका): carries the egg; fertilisation happens here.
    3. Uterus (गर्भाशय): the two oviducts join this elastic, bag-like organ.
    4. Cervix and vagina (योनि): the uterus opens into the vagina through the cervix.

    Fertilisation and implantation

    Exam point. The zygote implants in the thick lining of the uterus and keeps dividing to form the embryo.

    Sperms enter through the vagina and travel up to the oviduct, where they may meet the egg. Every month the uterus prepares for a zygote: its lining becomes thick, spongy and rich in blood.

    Placenta अपरा / प्लेसेंटा

    Exam point. A disc embedded in the uterine wall. It passes glucose and oxygen from mother to embryo and removes the embryo's wastes.

    Villi on the embryo's side give a large surface area. Blood spaces on the mother's side surround the villi. The baby develops in about nine months and is born by rhythmic contractions of the uterus muscles.

    Menstruation ऋतुस्राव

    Exam point. If the egg is not fertilised, it lives about one day. The thick uterine lining breaks down and comes out through the vagina as blood and mucus.

    The cycle repeats roughly every month and lasts about 2 to 8 days.

    Reproductive health

    Being healthy covers body, mind and society. Sexual acts can spread disease and cause unwanted pregnancy, so contraception and awareness matter.

    MethodExamplesHow it worksRemember
    Barrier (mechanical)Condom for the penis, covering devices for the vaginaStops sperm from reaching the eggAlso protects against STDs, including HIV-AIDS
    Hormonal (chemical)Oral pillsChange the hormone balance so eggs are not releasedCan have side effects
    IUCDCopper-T, loopPlaced in the uterus to prevent pregnancyMay cause side effects from irritation of the uterus
    SurgicalVasectomy (male), tubectomy (female)Vas deferens or fallopian tube is blockedPermanent; infection risk if not done properly

    Sexually transmitted diseases

    Bacterial: gonorrhoea, syphilis. Viral: warts, HIV-AIDS.

    Using a condom during sex helps prevent the transmission of many of these infections.

    Sex ratio and the law

    Exam point. Prenatal sex determination is prohibited by law in India.

    Misuse of surgical methods for the illegal removal of female foetuses (female foeticide) has reduced the child sex ratio sharply in some areas. A healthy society needs a balanced sex ratio.

    Why population control matters

    Exam point. Contraception lets couples plan the number and spacing of children, which supports the mother's health and the family's resources.
