Parts of the human eye

मानव नेत्र के भाग

The human eye works like a camera. It is a ball about 2.3 cm across. Light enters from the front, and a clear image forms on a screen at the back called the retina.

PartWhat it does
Cornea कॉर्निया / स्वच्छ मंडलThin, clear skin at the front of the eye. Light enters here. Most of the bending (refraction) of light happens at the cornea.
Iris परितारिकाDark muscle ring behind the cornea. It controls the size of the pupil. It also gives the eye its colour (black, brown, blue).
Pupil पुतलीSmall hole in the centre of the iris. It controls how much light enters the eye.
Eye lens अभिनेत्र लेंसA convex lens made of soft, jelly-like material. It does the fine adjustment of focus so the image falls exactly on the retina.
Ciliary muscles पक्ष्माभी पेशियाँHold the eye lens. They change the thickness of the lens, which changes its focal length.
Retina दृष्टिपटल / रेटिनाLight-sensitive screen at the back. The image forms here. It has rods (work in dim light) and cones (see colour and bright light).
Optic nerve दृक् तंत्रिकाCarries electrical signals from the retina to the brain.
Aqueous humour जलीय द्रवWatery liquid between the cornea and the lens.
Vitreous humour काचाभ द्रवClear jelly that fills the space between the lens and the retina. Keeps the eyeball round.
Sclera श्वेत पटलTough white outer cover. Protects the eye.
Blind spot अंध बिंदुPoint where the optic nerve leaves the eye. It has no rods or cones, so nothing is seen there.

How we see

हम कैसे देखते हैं

  1. Light from an object enters the eye through the cornea.
  2. The pupil lets in the right amount of light.
  3. The eye lens focuses the light on the retina.
  4. A real and inverted (उल्टा) image forms on the retina.
  5. Cells of the retina change light into electrical signals.
  6. The optic nerve carries these signals to the brain.
  7. The brain reads the signals and we see the object upright.

Pupil in bright and dim light

Bright light

The iris makes the pupil small, so less light enters and the eye is protected.

Dim light

The iris makes the pupil large, so more light enters and we can see better.

Board tip

"Which part controls the amount of light entering the eye?" Write pupil (its size is controlled by the iris). Writing both names gets full marks safely.

Power of accommodation

समंजन क्षमता

Definition

The ability of the eye lens to change its focal length, so that we can see both near and far objects clearly, is called the power of accommodation.

The ciliary muscles do this job by changing the thickness of the eye lens.

Looking atCiliary musclesEye lensFocal length
Far objectRelaxBecomes thinIncreases
Near objectContractBecomes thickDecreases

Near point and far point

Near point निकट बिंदु

The nearest point at which the eye sees clearly without strain. For a normal adult eye it is 25 cm. Also called the least distance of distinct vision (स्पष्ट दर्शन की न्यूनतम दूरी).

Far point दूर बिंदु

The farthest point up to which the eye sees clearly. For a normal eye it is infinity (∞).

So a normal eye sees clearly from 25 cm to infinity. This is its range of vision.

Board tip

"Why can't we read a book held very close to the eyes?" Because the focal length of the eye lens cannot be decreased below a certain limit. Objects closer than 25 cm cannot be focused on the retina.

Defects of vision and their correction

दृष्टि दोष तथा उनका संशोधन

Sometimes the eye cannot focus properly. There are three common refractive defects: myopia, hypermetropia and presbyopia. They are corrected with spectacle lenses. Cataract is a separate problem.

1. Myopia (near-sightedness) निकट दृष्टि दोष

  • Problem: Near objects are clear, but far objects look blurred.
  • The far point comes closer than infinity (for example, only up to 2 m).
  • Image forms in front of the retina.
  • Causes: (i) too much curvature of the eye lens (lens too thick), (ii) the eyeball becomes longer than normal.
  • Correction: a concave lens of suitable power. Its power is negative.

2. Hypermetropia (far-sightedness) दीर्घ दृष्टि दोष

  • Problem: Far objects are clear, but near objects look blurred.
  • The near point moves farther than 25 cm (for example, to 1 m).
  • Image forms behind the retina.
  • Causes: (i) focal length of the eye lens is too long (lens too thin), (ii) the eyeball becomes too small.
  • Correction: a convex lens of suitable power. Its power is positive.

3. Presbyopia जरा-दूरदृष्टिता

  • An old-age defect. The near point slowly moves away, so reading becomes hard.
  • Cause: the ciliary muscles become weak and the eye lens loses its flexibility, so the power of accommodation decreases.
  • Correction: convex lens. If a person has both myopia and hypermetropia, they use bifocal lenses (द्विफोकसी लेंस): the upper part is concave for far vision and the lower part is convex for reading.

4. Cataract मोतियाबिंद

  • In old age, the eye lens can become milky and cloudy.
  • This causes partial or complete loss of vision.
  • It is treated by cataract surgery, where the cloudy lens is replaced.

Today, refractive defects can also be corrected with contact lenses or surgery.

Memory trick
  • Myopia → Minus power → concave lens.
  • Hypermetropia → plus power → convex lens.
  • The Hindi name tells what you can see: निकट दृष्टि = sees near; दीर्घ दृष्टि = sees far.

Numericals made easy

आंकिक प्रश्न

Only two formulas are needed.

Power of lens:  P = 1 / f (f in metres)  → unit: dioptre (D)
Lens formula:  1/f = 1/v − 1/u
  • If f is in cm, use P = 100 / f.
  • Object distance u is always negative (object is on the left of the lens).
  • Concave lens → f and P are negative. Convex lens → f and P are positive.

For myopia

The concave lens must make a far object (at ∞) appear at the person's far point. So simply: f = − (far point distance).

Q1. A person with a myopic eye cannot see objects beyond 1.2 m clearly. What type of lens is needed, and what is its power?
Far point = 1.2 m
f = −1.2 m
P = 1/f = 1/(−1.2) = −0.83 D

Answer: Concave lens of power −0.83 D.

For hypermetropia

The convex lens must make an object at 25 cm appear at the person's near point. So u = −25 cm and v = −(defective near point).

Q2. The near point of a hypermetropic eye is 1 m. What is the power of the lens needed to correct it? (Normal near point = 25 cm.)
u = −25 cm, v = −100 cm
1/f = 1/v − 1/u
1/f = 1/(−100) − 1/(−25)
1/f = −1/100 + 4/100 = 3/100
f = 100/3 = 33.3 cm = 0.333 m
P = 100/f = 100/33.3 = +3.0 D

Answer: Convex lens of power +3.0 D.

Q3. A doctor prescribes −5.5 D for distant vision and +1.5 D for near vision. Find the focal lengths.
Distant vision: f = 1/P = 1/(−5.5) = −0.182 m = −18.2 cm
Near vision:   f = 1/P = 1/(+1.5) = +0.667 m = +66.7 cm

Answer: −18.2 cm (concave) and +66.7 cm (convex).

Board tip

Always write the sign (+ or −) with the power and say which lens it is. Marks are cut for a missing sign.

Prism and dispersion of light

प्रिज़्म तथा प्रकाश का वर्ण विक्षेपण

A glass prism has two slanted faces. Light bends (refracts) twice: once when it enters and once when it leaves. The angle by which the light turns from its original path is the angle of deviation.

Definition

The splitting of white light into its seven colours is called dispersion (वर्ण विक्षेपण). The band of colours is called a spectrum.

Violet Indigo Blue Green Yellow Orange Red

Why does white light split?

Each colour travels at a different speed inside glass, so each colour bends by a different angle. Red bends least, violet bends most.

Newton's experiment: recombination

Isaac Newton placed a second, identical prism upside down after the first. The seven colours joined again and white light came out. This proved that white light is made of seven colours.

Rainbow इंद्रधनुष

  • A natural spectrum seen in the sky after rain.
  • Tiny water drops in the air act like small prisms.
  • Inside each drop, light goes through refraction and dispersion, then internal reflection, then refraction again as it comes out.
  • A rainbow is always seen in the direction opposite to the Sun (Sun behind you).

Atmospheric refraction

वायुमंडलीय अपवर्तन

Air is not the same everywhere. Hot air is lighter (rarer) and cool air is denser. Light bends as it passes through these different layers. This bending of light by the Earth's atmosphere is called atmospheric refraction.

Example: Objects seen through the hot air above a fire or a hot road seem to shake or waver.

Twinkling of stars तारों का टिमटिमाना

  • Starlight bends again and again as it enters the atmosphere.
  • Air keeps moving, so the amount of bending keeps changing.
  • The star's apparent position and brightness keep changing, so the star seems to twinkle.
  • Because of refraction, a star also looks slightly higher than its real position.
Board tip

Why don't planets twinkle? Planets are much closer, so they look like a small disc (many points of light), not a single point. The changes from all these points cancel out, so the light looks steady.

Early sunrise and late sunset

  • We see the Sun about 2 minutes before it actually rises above the horizon.
  • We keep seeing it for about 2 minutes after it actually sets.
  • Reason: atmospheric refraction bends sunlight towards us even when the Sun is just below the horizon.
  • So the day becomes about 4 minutes longer.

Scattering of light

प्रकाश का प्रकीर्णन

When light hits tiny particles (dust, smoke, water drops, air molecules), it spreads out in many directions. This is scattering.

Tyndall effect · टिंडल प्रभाव

When a beam of light passes through a medium with tiny particles, its path becomes visible because the particles scatter the light. Examples: sunlight entering a dense forest through gaps, or a beam of light in a smoky or dusty room.

The colour we see depends on the size of the particles:

  • Very small particles scatter mostly blue light (short wavelength).
  • Bigger particles scatter longer wavelengths (red side).
  • Very large particles scatter all colours equally, so the light looks white.

Everyday questions

QuestionSimple reason
Why is the sky blue?Air molecules are very small. They scatter blue light much more than red. This scattered blue light reaches our eyes from all directions.
Why does the sky look dark to astronauts?At very high altitude there is almost no air, so there is no scattering.
Why is the Sun red at sunrise and sunset?Sunlight travels a longer path through air. Most blue light gets scattered away on the way. Mainly red light reaches our eyes.
Why are danger signals red?Red light is scattered the least by fog and smoke, so it can be seen from far away.
Why are clouds white?Water drops in clouds are large. They scatter all colours equally, which together look white.

Short answer

What is meant by power of accommodation of the eye?2 marks

The ability of the eye lens to adjust its focal length to see near and far objects clearly. For far objects the ciliary muscles relax and the lens becomes thin. For near objects they contract and the lens becomes thick.

Why do stars twinkle?2 marks

Starlight is refracted again and again by the moving layers of the atmosphere. Its apparent position and brightness keep changing, so the star seems to twinkle.

Why is the colour of the clear sky blue?2 marks

Air molecules are smaller than the wavelength of visible light. They scatter blue light (short wavelength) much more than red, so blue light reaches our eyes from all over the sky.

Why is red colour used for danger signals?2 marks

Red light has the longest wavelength and is scattered the least by fog, smoke and dust. So it can be seen clearly from a long distance.

What is presbyopia? How is it corrected?2 marks

An old-age defect in which the near point moves away because the ciliary muscles weaken and the lens loses flexibility. It is corrected by a convex lens, or by bifocal lenses if the person also has myopia.
