10.1

The human eye मानव नेत्र

The eye works like a camera. Light enters through the cornea, the eye lens focuses it, and a real, inverted image forms on the retina. The retina's cells send electrical signals through the optic nerve, and the brain turns the image upright. Select any part to see what it does.

Retina

दृष्टिपटल

Power of accommodation Often asked

The ability of the eye lens to adjust its focal length is called the power of accommodation (समंजन क्षमता). The ciliary muscles change the curvature of the lens.

  • Distant object: ciliary muscles relax, lens becomes thin, focal length increases.
  • Near object: ciliary muscles contract, lens becomes thick, focal length decreases.
  • The focal length cannot decrease below a certain minimum. That is why we cannot see things clearly closer than 25 cm.
  • When an object moves farther away, the image distance stays the same. Only the focal length changes, so the image stays on the retina.

Rods and cones

  • Rods respond to the intensity of light (dim-light vision).
  • Cones respond to colour. They need bright light.
  • The pupil widens in dim light and narrows in bright light. That is why we cannot see for a moment after entering a dark room from sunlight.
  • Two eyes give a wider field of view (about 180° instead of 150°), better detection of faint objects and a sense of depth (3D vision).

Eye donation: corneal blindness can be cured by transplanting a donated cornea. Eyes must be removed within 4 to 6 hours of death, and one pair of eyes can give vision to up to four corneal-blind people. Anyone can donate, whatever their age or sex, including people who wear spectacles.

10.3

Refraction of light through a glass prism काँच के प्रिज़्म से प्रकाश का अपवर्तन

A prism has two triangular bases and three rectangular faces. The two faces that light passes through are inclined to each other, unlike a glass slab where they are parallel.

  • Angle of prism (A): the angle between its two refracting faces.
  • Angle of deviation (δ): the angle between the incident ray (extended) and the emergent ray.
  • The ray bends towards the base of the prism at both faces.
  • Relation: i + e = A + δ
10.4

Dispersion of white light श्वेत प्रकाश का विक्षेपण

Dispersion is the splitting of white light into its seven constituent colours. The band of colours is called a spectrum. Each colour bends by a different angle in glass: red bends the least and violet bends the most.

Rainbow इंद्रधनुष Often asked

A rainbow is a natural spectrum seen in the sky after a rain shower. Tiny water droplets in the air act like small prisms.

  1. Sunlight enters a droplet and is refracted and dispersed.
  2. It is internally reflected from the back of the droplet.
  3. It is refracted again as it comes out, and the colours spread further.

A rainbow always forms in the direction opposite to the Sun. You can also see one through a waterfall or fountain on a sunny day with the Sun behind you.

10.5

Atmospheric refraction वायुमंडलीय अपवर्तन

The air is not uniform. Its density and temperature change with height, so its refractive index changes too. Light passing through it bends gradually. This is atmospheric refraction. Air just above a fire or a hot road is lighter than the air above it, so objects seen through it appear to waver.

Star vs planet

Star (point source)
Planet (extended source)

With an atmosphere, starlight keeps bending by changing amounts, so the star flickers. The planet stays steady.

Twinkling of stars Often asked

  • Starlight is refracted continuously as it enters the atmosphere. Because the air keeps changing, the star's apparent position and brightness keep fluctuating. It twinkles.
  • A star is very far away, so it behaves like a point source.
  • A star near the horizon appears slightly higher than its actual position.

Why planets do not twinkle

Planets are much closer, so they act as extended sources: a collection of many point sources. Their brightness changes average out to zero.

Advance sunrise and delayed sunset

We see the Sun about 2 minutes before it actually rises and about 2 minutes after it actually sets. The day is about 4 minutes longer. Refraction also makes the Sun's disc look flattened at sunrise and sunset.

10.6

Scattering of light प्रकाश का प्रकीर्णन

Tyndall effect टिंडल प्रभाव

When a beam of light passes through a colloid, its path becomes visible because the particles scatter the light. Examples: sunlight through the canopy of a dense forest, and a beam through a smoke-filled room.

The colour of scattered light depends on particle size:

  • Very fine particles scatter mainly blue light (short wavelength).
  • Larger particles scatter longer wavelengths.
  • Large enough particles scatter all colours equally, so the scattered light looks white. This is why clouds are white.

Why is the sky blue? Often asked

Air molecules are smaller than the wavelength of visible light. They scatter blue light (shorter wavelength) much more than red. Red light has a wavelength about 1.8 times that of blue. The scattered blue light reaches our eyes from all directions.

Without an atmosphere there would be no scattering, so the sky would look dark. Astronauts flying very high see a dark sky.

Danger signals are red

Red light is scattered the least by fog and smoke, so it can be seen from far away.

Important questions महत्त्वपूर्ण प्रश्न

Write your answer first. Then open the model answer.

Name the parts of the human eye and give the function of each.Long answer
  • Cornea: transparent front bulge; most of the refraction happens at its outer surface.
  • Iris: dark muscular diaphragm that controls the size of the pupil.
  • Pupil: opening that regulates the amount of light entering the eye.
  • Eye lens: convex, jelly-like lens; fine-adjusts focal length to form a real, inverted image on the retina.
  • Ciliary muscles: change the curvature of the lens (accommodation).
  • Retina: light-sensitive screen with rods and cones; produces electrical signals.
  • Optic nerve: carries the signals to the brain.

Draw a labelled diagram with these seven parts.

What is myopia? State two causes. How is it corrected? Draw ray diagrams.Long answer

Myopia (near-sightedness): a person can see nearby objects clearly but not distant ones. The far point is nearer than infinity, and the image of a distant object forms in front of the retina.

Causes: (i) excessive curvature of the eye lens, (ii) elongation of the eyeball.

Correction: a concave lens of suitable power. It diverges the rays so the image forms on the retina.

Diagrams: (a) far point of myopic eye, (b) myopic eye, (c) corrected with concave lens. Use the simulator above to see all three.

What is hypermetropia? State two causes and the correction.Long answer

Hypermetropia (far-sightedness): a person can see distant objects clearly but not nearby ones. The near point is farther than 25 cm, and the image of a near object forms behind the retina.

Causes: (i) the focal length of the eye lens is too long, (ii) the eyeball has become too small.

Correction: a convex lens of suitable power, which adds converging power.

A person with a myopic eye cannot see objects beyond 1.2 m distinctly. What type of corrective lens should be used? Find its power.Numerical

Far point = 1.2 m Concave lens with f = −(far point) = −1.2 m P = 1/f = 1/(−1.2) = −0.83 D

A concave lens of power −0.83 D.

The far point of a myopic person is 80 cm in front of the eye. What is the nature and power of the lens required?Numerical

f = −80 cm = −0.8 m P = 1/f = 1/(−0.8) = −1.25 D

A concave lens of power −1.25 D.

The near point of a hypermetropic eye is 1 m. What is the power of the lens required? (Normal near point = 25 cm)Numerical

u = −25 cm, v = −100 cm 1/f = 1/v − 1/u = −1/100 + 1/25 = 3/100 f = 33.3 cm = 0.333 m P = 1/f = +3.0 D

A convex lens of power +3.0 D.

A student cannot read the blackboard from the last row. What defect could it be, and how is it corrected?Short answer

The student has myopia. It is corrected with spectacles that have a concave lens of suitable power.

Explain the formation of a rainbow with a diagram.Long answer

After rain, tiny water droplets in the air act as small prisms. Sunlight entering a droplet is refracted and dispersed, then internally reflected at the back of the droplet, and refracted again as it leaves. The different colours reach the observer's eye at different angles, so a band of seven colours is seen. A rainbow always forms opposite to the Sun.

Why do stars twinkle but planets do not?Short answer

Starlight undergoes continuous atmospheric refraction. The star is a point source, so small changes in the path make its position and brightness flicker. Planets are closer and act as extended sources; the variations from their many points cancel out, so they do not twinkle.

What is the Tyndall effect? Give two examples.Short answer

The scattering of light by colloidal particles, which makes the path of a light beam visible. Examples: sunlight entering a smoke-filled room through a small hole, and sunlight passing through the canopy of a dense forest.

A person needs a lens of power −5.5 D for distant vision and +1.5 D for near vision. Find the focal lengths.Numerical

Distant vision: f = 1/P = 1/(−5.5) m = −0.182 m = −18.2 cm Near vision: f = 1/P = 1/(+1.5) m = +0.667 m = +66.7 cm

He or she needs a bifocal lens.

Why does the Sun look reddish at sunrise and sunset but white at noon?Short answer

Near the horizon, sunlight crosses a much thicker layer of air. Most of the blue light is scattered away on the way, so mainly red and orange light reach our eyes. At noon the path is short and little blue is scattered, so the Sun looks white.

Formulas and glossary सूत्र और शब्दावली

P = 1 / f

Power in dioptre (D), f in metres. Convex lens: P positive. Concave lens: P negative.

1/v − 1/u = 1/f

Lens formula. Use the sign convention: distances to the left of the lens are negative.

f = −(far point)

Myopia. The lens makes a distant object's image at the person's far point.

f = 25d / (d − 25)

Hypermetropia, in cm. d is the person's near point; u = −25 cm, v = −d.

i + e = A + δ

Prism: angle of incidence plus emergence equals angle of prism plus deviation.

P = P₁ + P₂ + …

Lenses in contact: powers add up.
